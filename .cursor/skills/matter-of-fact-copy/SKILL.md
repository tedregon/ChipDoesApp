# Matter-of-Fact Copy

## Purpose
Write copy that states what happened, what was built, and what it did—without selling, flattering, or performing enthusiasm.

Default voice for designwithchip.com unless the user asks for something else.

## Tone Definition
Matter-of-fact means:
- Plain language. Say the thing directly.
- Facts before feelings. Lead with role, scope, and outcome.
- Specific over impressive. Numbers, products, and deliverables beat adjectives.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence where possible.
- Neutral confidence. No need to convince the reader you are good—they can infer it from the work.

## Do
- Name the company, role, and timeframe when relevant.
- State what you did and what changed (metrics when available).
- Use active verbs: built, led, shipped, designed, reduced, grew.
- Keep paragraphs to 2–4 sentences.
- Let results speak. "Onboarding dropped from 40 days to 9" needs no embellishment.

## Avoid
- Hype: revolutionary, game-changing, passionate, obsessed, cutting-edge, world-class.
- Empty claims: bridging gaps, driving impact, unlocking potential, shaping the future.
- Marketing filler: "designed to," "built for people who," "the tool you need."
- Performative humility or false casualness.
- Stacking adjectives or metaphors.
- Explaining why the reader should be impressed—show the work instead.

## Sentence Patterns
Prefer:
- `[Role], [Company] — [what you did]. [specific outcome].`
- `[Product] — [what it does]. [one concrete detail].`
- Short declarative statements. No rhetorical questions on bio or experience copy.

## Before / After

**Too promotional:**
> I've spent over a decade bridging what users need with what the business requires, shipping global products that make a real impact.

**Matter-of-fact:**
> Twelve years in product design. Lead and senior IC roles at Jigsaw and Checkout.com. I also build and ship my own apps.

---

**Too promotional:**
> I grew the design team and built transformative products for private equity firms—including an AI-powered Timelines tool that revolutionized how teams work.

**Matter-of-fact:**
> Design Lead, Jigsaw. Grew the team from two contractors to five full-time designers. Shipped a data platform for private equity firms and an AI Timelines tool that pulls events from legal documents.

---

**Too promotional:**
> Five years shaping core merchant products at one of Europe's largest fintechs, driving meaningful outcomes for global clients.

**Matter-of-fact:**
> Senior Product Designer, Checkout.com. Five years on merchant products: KYC/KYB onboarding (40 days to 9), card issuance, the Merchant Hub, and fraud and dispute tooling.

## Page-Type Guidance

### Experience / bio sections
- Open with scope (years, domains), not a mission statement.
- One short paragraph per role. Role title in bold or as a subhead.
- End with what you do now—one sentence.

### Product pages
- First line: what the product is and who it's for.
- Mechanics and rules in plain language (see WordJam page as reference).
- Skip emotional setup unless the product's value is inherently emotional (e.g. Acting Coach can be slightly warmer; still no hype).

### Mentorship / Train page
- State the problem plainly. State what the sessions cover.
- Testimonials carry emotion; your copy should not compete with them.

## Rewrite Workflow
1. Read the current copy and highlight hype, vagueness, and repetition.
2. Extract facts: roles, dates, products, metrics, deliverables.
3. Rewrite in short declarative sentences. Cut every sentence that doesn't add a fact.
4. Read aloud. If it sounds like a LinkedIn post or a landing page template, cut again.
5. Check consistency with existing site copy and terminology.

## QA Checklist
- [ ] Every paragraph contains at least one concrete fact (role, product, number, or deliverable).
- [ ] No banned hype words unless quoting someone else.
- [ ] No sentence exists only to praise or summarize impressiveness.
- [ ] A stranger could skim in 30 seconds and know what you did.
- [ ] Tone matches across sections on the same page.
